## Why the sun matters

Ultraviolet (UV) light from the sun is the main cause of sun spots (dark spots), uneven skin tone and early signs of ageing such as fine lines and loss of firmness. It also raises the risk of skin cancer. In a sunny climate like Egypt's, UV levels are high for much of the year.

## How to use sunscreen well

- Choose a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher.
- Apply enough: about a teaspoon for the face and neck. Most people use too little.
- Apply it about 15 minutes before going out, and reapply every two hours outdoors and after swimming, sweating or towel drying.
- Don't forget the ears, neck and the backs of the hands.

## Beyond sunscreen

Seek shade, especially between 10 am and 4 pm; wear a wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses; and consider clothing that covers the skin. Tanning beds damage the skin just like the sun.

## Spots to get checked

Most dark spots from the sun are harmless, but a dermatologist should check any spot that is new and growing, has an irregular border or several colours, is bigger than about 6 mm, bleeds, itches or doesn't heal, or looks different from your other spots. Getting changing spots checked early matters.
