## What melasma is

Melasma causes brown or grey-brown patches, usually on the cheeks, forehead, nose and upper lip. It is more common in women and in people with medium to darker skin tones, including many people in Egypt and the Middle East. It is not contagious and not dangerous, but it can be persistent and often returns.

## What makes it worse

Sunlight is the main trigger; even visible light and heat can darken patches. Hormonal changes also play a role, for example pregnancy (sometimes called the "mask of pregnancy") and hormonal contraception. Irritating products and home remedies, such as lemon juice or harsh scrubs, can inflame the skin and make pigmentation darker.

## What helps

Daily, careful sun protection is the foundation of every melasma treatment:

- A broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher every day, including cloudy days and indoors near windows. Tinted sunscreens also help block visible light.
- Reapply every two hours outdoors, and after sweating or washing the face.
- A wide-brimmed hat and shade, especially at midday.
- Gentle, fragrance-free skin care.

## When to see a dermatologist

Other conditions can look like melasma, so a dermatologist should confirm it before treatment. They can plan treatment that fits your skin type; some treatments aren't suitable during pregnancy or breastfeeding, and some procedures can worsen pigmentation if not chosen carefully. Any spot that is changing, bleeding or looks different from the rest should be checked promptly.
