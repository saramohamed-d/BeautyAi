## Normal shedding

Losing around 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal. Hair grows in cycles, and a resting hair falls out before a new one grows in its place.

## Common causes of increased hair loss

- **Shedding after a stressful event:** a high fever or illness, surgery, childbirth, crash dieting or major stress can cause widespread shedding that typically starts two to three months later. It usually improves on its own within months.
- **Pattern hair loss:** gradual thinning, often at the crown or the parting, which tends to run in families.
- **Medical causes:** thyroid problems, low iron and some other nutritional deficiencies, and hormonal conditions.
- **Patchy hair loss:** smooth round bald patches can be a sign of an autoimmune condition (alopecia areata).
- **Hair styling:** tight braids, ponytails and extensions can pull hair out over time, especially at the hairline.

## When to see a doctor

See a dermatologist if hair loss is sudden, comes out in patches, is getting worse, or comes with itching, scaling, redness or pain on the scalp. Also see a doctor if it comes with other symptoms such as tiredness, weight change or irregular periods. A dermatologist can examine the scalp and may suggest blood tests to find the cause; treatment depends on the cause.

## Gentle hair care

Avoid tight hairstyles, handle wet hair gently, and limit heat styling and harsh chemical treatments while the cause is being checked.
