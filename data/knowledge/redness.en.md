## Causes of facial redness

A red face can come from many things: blushing, irritation from skin products, sunburn, eczema, acne, and rosacea. Rosacea is a long-term condition that causes flushing, persistent redness across the cheeks and nose, visible small blood vessels, and sometimes bumps that can look like acne. It can also affect the eyes, making them dry, gritty or irritated.

## Common rosacea triggers

Triggers differ from person to person. Common ones include sun exposure, heat and hot weather, hot drinks, spicy food, alcohol, strenuous exercise, stress, and harsh or fragranced skin products. Keeping a simple diary can help you find your own triggers.

## Gentle care

- Use mild, fragrance-free cleansers and moisturisers.
- Avoid scrubs and products that sting or burn.
- Use daily sunscreen; mineral sunscreens are often better tolerated by sensitive skin.
- Avoid very hot water on the face.

## When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if redness is persistent, keeps coming back, comes with bumps or thickened skin, or affects your eyes (an eye problem should be checked promptly). Several conditions look alike, and the right treatment depends on the diagnosis.
