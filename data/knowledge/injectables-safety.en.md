## What these treatments do

Botulinum toxin injections (often called "Botox") temporarily relax specific muscles to soften expression lines, such as frown lines. Dermal fillers, most commonly made of hyaluronic acid, add volume to areas such as the lips or cheeks. Both are medical procedures and should be done by a qualified, licensed doctor.

## Before treatment

Choose a licensed doctor in a proper clinic, and ask what product will be used. Tell the doctor about your medicines and supplements, allergies, previous treatments, and any medical conditions. These treatments are generally not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

## Normal after-effects

Mild redness, swelling, tenderness and bruising at the injection sites are common and usually settle within a few days. Follow your doctor's aftercare instructions; common advice includes not rubbing or massaging the area and avoiding strenuous exercise and heat for about a day.

## Warning signs: get help urgently

A rare but serious complication of fillers is a blocked blood vessel. Contact the treating doctor **immediately** and go to an emergency department (call 123 in Egypt) if you notice, especially within hours of a filler:

- skin that turns white, pale, blue, grey or blotchy near the treated area,
- severe or increasing pain,
- any change in vision, or
- difficulty breathing or swelling of the lips, tongue or throat (possible allergic reaction).

Also contact the doctor promptly for signs of infection (increasing redness, warmth, swelling or pus, or fever), or if an eyelid or eyebrow droops after a toxin injection.
