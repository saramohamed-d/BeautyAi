## What acne is

Acne is one of the most common skin conditions. It happens when hair follicles become clogged with oil and dead skin cells. Bacteria that normally live on the skin and inflammation can then turn these clogged pores into spots (pimples).

Acne can appear as blackheads and whiteheads, small red bumps, pus-filled spots, or deeper painful lumps (nodules and cysts). It is most common on the face, chest and back.

## Common triggers

Hormonal changes (puberty, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy), genetics, some cosmetics and hair products, and certain medicines can all play a part. Stress can make existing acne worse. Picking or squeezing pimples increases inflammation and the risk of marks and scars.

## Everyday habits that help

- Wash the face gently twice a day and after sweating. Harsh scrubbing irritates the skin.
- Choose products labelled "non-comedogenic" or "oil-free".
- Avoid picking or squeezing pimples.
- Use sunscreen every day; many acne treatments make the skin more sensitive to the sun.

Treatments usually take 6 to 12 weeks to show results, so consistency matters.

## When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if acne is painful or deep, leaves dark marks or scars, has not improved after two to three months of good skin care, or affects how you feel. Sudden acne in adulthood together with irregular periods or extra facial or body hair can have a hormonal cause worth checking. A dermatologist can confirm the type of acne and recommend treatment suited to your skin, including options that are safe during pregnancy.
